## Task Overview

You are given:
- A **user text**
- Two explainers (**Explainer A and Explainer B**) analyzing Conscientiousness

Your task:
1. Compare both explainers across Emotion, Cognition, and Sociality
2. Evaluate which explainer is more grounded in the text
3. Output final classification: **HIGH or LOW Conscientiousness**

---

# 1. Comparative Analysis (A vs B)

For each element:

- Identify agreements and disagreements
- Check alignment with **exact phrases from the user text**
- Evaluate depth, specificity, and evidence quality
- Penalize over-interpretation and projection

---

## A. Emotion

Compare how each explainer interprets emotional signals related to conscientious behavior:
- responsibility-related emotions (guilt, pride, frustration)
- motivation vs apathy toward tasks
- emotional response to obligations

### 🚫 Critical Anti-Error Rule (Emotion)

Do NOT treat the following as HIGH or LOW Conscientiousness by themselves:
- mild stress or frustration
- casual regret (“I should have…”)
- nostalgia or reflection
- temporary laziness or tiredness
- mood fluctuations in stream-of-consciousness writing

👉 These are **non-diagnostic unless tied to sustained behavior patterns**

---

## B. Cognition

Compare:
- goal-directed thinking vs drifting
- planning vs absence of planning
- consistency of attention to tasks

### 🚫 Critical Anti-Error Rule (Cognition)

Do NOT interpret as Conscientiousness signals:
- stream-of-consciousness writing style
- topic switching or associative thinking
- environmental distraction in text
- informal or unstructured narrative flow
- mention of multiple activities or thoughts

👉 These are **format artifacts, not trait indicators**

---

## C. Sociality

Compare:
- responsibility toward others (duties, obligations, commitments)
- follow-through in social/work expectations
- reliability in interpersonal context

### 🚫 Critical Anti-Error Rule (Sociality)

Do NOT treat as HIGH or LOW Conscientiousness:
- frequency of social thoughts
- desire for friends or relationships
- casual mention of others
- social comparison or relationship focus

👉 Only consider sociality when it reflects **duty, obligation, or reliability behavior**

---

# 2. Core Conscientiousness Decision Rules (CRITICAL)

Trait must be inferred from:

> **relationship to duty, obligation, and self-regulation under expectation**

NOT from:
- writing structure
- topic randomness
- emotional tone
- verbosity or coherence

---

# 🔴 HIGH Conscientiousness Criteria (must satisfy multiple signals)

Classify as HIGH ONLY if at least **2+ strong indicators are present**:

## 1. Internalized Duty System
- explicit guilt about not meeting responsibilities
- strong self-correction pressure (“I must fix this”, “I need to do better”)
- persistent awareness of obligations as moral pressure

## 2. Structured Self-Regulation
- clear planning or prioritization systems
- consistent attempt to organize time/tasks
- repeated return to task completion strategy

## 3. Execution Orientation
- linking intentions to concrete action steps
- sustained focus on completing obligations
- evidence of follow-through mindset, not just thoughts

---

# 🟢 LOW Conscientiousness Criteria (must show stability of drift or low duty pressure)

Classify as LOW if the text shows:

## 1. Low Duty Pressure
- absence of guilt-driven correction loops
- weak or absent sense of obligation urgency
- acceptance of “just getting by”

## 2. Drift-Based Attention
- attention shifts without corrective effort
- task mentions are brief and not operationalized
- no sustained prioritization system

## 3. Passive Task Relationship
- obligations are mentioned but not structured into action
- goals remain abstract or situational
- no consistent execution tracking

---

# 🚫 CRITICAL FALSE POSITIVE RULE (IMPORTANT)

Do NOT infer HIGH Conscientiousness from:
- mentioning plans or intentions (“I will do homework later”)
- awareness of deadlines or time
- saying “I should” or “I need to”
- brief guilt or regret statements

👉 These are **weak signals unless repeatedly converted into structured action**

---

# 3. Decision Calibration Rule

- If HIGH indicators are weak, isolated, or non-repetitive → choose LOW
- If evidence is mixed → prefer LOW (avoid over-attribution)
- Ignore writing style and focus only on **duty-to-action consistency**

---

# 4. Overall Evaluation

Choose which explainer (A or B) better reflects the actual trait in the text:

Criteria:
- grounded reasoning
- correct interpretation of duty vs intention
- avoidance of over-interpretation
- correct handling of stream-of-consciousness noise

---

# 5. Final Output

Return exactly ONE label:

- HIGH
- LOW

---

# Input

Text: {text}

Explainer A: {explain_1}

Explainer B: {explain_2}

## Task: Compare Two Explainers and Classify Neuroticism

You are given:
- A user text
- Explainer A and Explainer B analyzing Neuroticism

Your job:
1. Compare both explainers
2. Evaluate which is more accurate and grounded in the text
3. Output a final classification: **HIGH or LOW Neuroticism**

---

# 1. Comparative Analysis (A vs B)

Analyze the following three dimensions:

---

## A. Emotion

Compare:
- emotional intensity
- emotional stability vs fluctuation
- presence of distress, negativity, or calmness

### 🚫 Critical rule (avoid false HIGH triggers)

Do NOT treat the following as HIGH Neuroticism by themselves:
- mild sadness or nostalgia
- irritation or frustration in isolation
- self-reflection or introspection
- casual worry or uncertainty
- social comparison

These are **LOW-compatible or neutral emotional states unless they escalate or dominate the text**

---

## B. Cognition

Compare:
- thought organization (structured vs scattered)
- presence of rumination loops
- self-referential negative spirals

### 🚫 Critical rule

Do NOT interpret as neuroticism:
- stream-of-consciousness writing
- topic switching or associative thinking
- planning lists or daily scheduling
- casual introspection or reflection

Only consider cognition as HIGH Neuroticism if:
- thoughts repeatedly return to distressing interpretations
- there is escalation of negative thinking across sentences
- cognition is clearly driven by emotional instability

---

## C. Sociality

Compare:
- social comparison patterns
- belonging needs
- interpersonal tension or sensitivity

### 🚫 Critical rule

Do NOT treat as HIGH Neuroticism:
- desire for friends, belonging, or connection
- comparing oneself to others
- mild jealousy, admiration, or insecurity

Only treat as HIGH if:
- social comparison is obsessive or self-devaluing
- interpersonal thoughts trigger sustained distress
- relationships are framed as emotionally destabilizing

---

# 2. Symmetric Trait Decision Rules (VERY IMPORTANT)

You must evaluate HIGH and LOW Neuroticism symmetrically.

---

## 🔴 HIGH Neuroticism requires ALL of the following:

At least **2 or more strong indicators**:

- sustained negative emotional intensity (not brief or mild)
- emotional escalation or spiraling across sentences
- cognitive rumination loops driven by distress
- catastrophic or self-devaluing interpretations
- inability to stabilize emotional state within the text

 IMPORTANT:
Do NOT classify as HIGH based on:
- isolated frustration
- mild sadness
- disorganized writing alone
- normal life stress or reflection

---

##  LOW Neuroticism is defined by POSITIVE STABILITY SIGNALS:

LOW must be supported by evidence of:

- emotionally neutral or mildly negative tone without escalation
- ability to shift topics without distress spirals
- presence of reflection without emotional overwhelm
- normal social comparison without self-devaluation loops
- absence of sustained negative cognitive-emotional feedback loops

 IMPORTANT:
LOW is NOT just “absence of HIGH signals”
LOW must show **emotional and cognitive stability patterns**

---

# 3. Decision Calibration Rule

- If HIGH indicators are weak, isolated, or ambiguous → choose LOW
- Treat uncertainty language (“I guess”, “maybe”, “I wonder”) as neutral
- Do NOT infer neuroticism from normal introspection or planning behavior

---

# 4. Overall Evaluation

Decide:
- Which explainer (A or B) better reflects the actual trait in the text

Criteria:
- grounded reasoning
- correct use of evidence
- avoidance of over-interpretation

---

# 5. Final Output

Return exactly ONE label:

- HIGH
- LOW

---

# Input Format

Text: {text}

Explainer A: {explain_1}

Explainer B: {explain_2}
